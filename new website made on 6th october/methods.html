Reproducible workflow

Methods and control logic

Time alignment, calibration, reference management and bounded actuation are treated as part of the measurement—not implementation detail.

1. Synchronous measurement

Detector centroids, interferometric OPL, TEC setpoint and measured temperature, pressure, humidity and regional thermal sensors are resampled onto a common time axis. Raw and smoothed channels are retained separately. Clock offsets, missing intervals and warm-up behaviour are checked before correlation or controller scoring.

ΔOPL(t) = OPL(t) − OPL(t₀)    |    Δx(t), Δy(t) = centroid(t) − reference

2. Calibration

Active-optics calibration estimates detector displacement per command direction and constructs a 2×2 response matrix. Direction labels and return-to-centre behaviour are verified explicitly; the 2026 calibration identified an initial east/west command-label swap that was corrected before interpretation.

[Δx, Δy]ᵀ = A · [u₁, u₂]ᵀ    ⇒    u = A⁻¹e

The most recent 100- and 1000-step measurements are internally consistent at approximately 0.019–0.021 µm per step. Their difference from the 2024 calibration requires hardware/configuration confirmation before it is described as an absolute precision improvement.

3. Environmental model

Rolling ordinary least squares and physically motivated environmental terms are used to estimate slow OPL drift. A reported training fit of R² = 0.972, RMSE = 1.52 and MAE = 1.16 describes one training dataset only; it is not an out-of-sample validation result.

OPL̂(t) = β₀ + βTT(t) + βPP(t) + βHH(t) + …

4. TEC-primary hybrid control

The thermal loop absorbs slow offsets and preserves active-optics travel. The AO loop applies small, bounded corrections only when residual detector error exceeds a deadband. Reference management prevents a convenient endpoint from being mistaken for sustained regulation.

  1. Validate acquisition and reference state.
  2. Estimate slow drift from thermal, OPL and environmental channels.
  3. Apply rate-limited TEC correction.
  4. Transform remaining centroid error through the AO calibration matrix.
  5. Clamp per-command and cumulative AO travel.
  6. Score the full run and defined feedback intervals separately.
