Instrument architecture

A compact optical system with control designed in.

EXOhSPEC is a fibre-fed, double-pass high-resolution spectrograph. The optical bench, fibre feed, acquisition unit, environmental enclosure and software pipeline form one measurement system.

01 / Optical design

Folded double-pass train

Optical and mechanical development share the same stability envelope.

Zemax optimisation defines the folded beam path; SolidWorks translates it into a compact opto-mechanical layout. NARIT designed, manufactured and tested the achromatic triplet used in the camera system.

02 / Fibre systems

Illumination stability

The fibre link is an optical subsystem, not a cable.

Simulation and bench measurements compare tapered geometries, injection angles and modal behaviour. Optical agitation using a galvanometer was later evaluated as a route to mitigating modal noise.

Model

Tapered-fibre propagation

COMSOL calculations examine different input beams between 0° and 17° and compare larger and smaller taper geometries.

03 / Active control

Measure and correct

Arc-line motion provides an image-plane stability signal.

The established system measures spectral-line centroids and commands a piezo active-optics unit. The original programme site reports metrology reaching 0.017 pixel. The later CLFCD track extends this work with environmental telemetry, optical-path metrology and hybrid control.

04 / Acquisition & guiding

Star to fibre

Guiding keeps the target image on the fibre core.

The acquisition and guiding unit images the target star and focused fibre face simultaneously. Telescope focus and pointing place the star on the darker fibre core; the internal camera supports closed-loop telescope guiding.

Acquire the target, focus the stellar image, place it on the recorded fibre position, maximise spectrograph flux and maintain the alignment.

Thai National Observatory

From the Hatfield laboratory to AstroPark and the TNT.

The programme connects the University of Hertfordshire laboratory to NARIT facilities. At AstroPark, the spectrograph is housed on the ground floor with telescope control above; the instrument was developed as a prototype for the 2.4 m Thai National Telescope.
