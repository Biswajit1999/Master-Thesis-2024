University of Hertfordshire · Precision spectroscopy

Controlling environmental drift in a high-resolution spectrograph

EXOhSPEC is an experimental platform for studying how temperature, pressure and humidity perturb optical path length and detector-plane position—and how bounded thermal and active-optics feedback can reduce that motion.

One system, four coupled layers

The research is organised around the causal chain from ambient conditions to optical-path change, detector motion and actuator response. Each layer has its own telemetry, calibration and uncertainty.

Measurement

Environmental metrology

Temperature, pressure and humidity are time-aligned with interferometer and detector data to identify common drift and lag.

Measurement stack →
Model

OPL prediction

Rolling regression and physically motivated sensitivity terms are evaluated as feed-forward signals, with training-fit metrics kept distinct from out-of-sample performance.

Model boundary →
Control

Hybrid stabilisation

Slow drift is assigned to the TEC loop; active optics corrects small residual image motion while cumulative travel is monitored.

Evidence language

A scientific instrument site should make the status of every statement legible. This record uses four labels throughout.

Measured

Direct experiment

A value recomputed from an identified run or reported from traceable telemetry.

Modelled

Fit or prediction

A relationship estimated from data; training fit is not presented as generalisation evidence.

Proposed

Design awaiting validation

An engineering intervention—such as the V25 fan concept—not yet verified on the instrument.

Interpretation boundary. A final point near zero is not evidence of continuous fine lock. Performance is reported using time-in-band, RMS, bias, intervention count and the interval over which each metric was evaluated.
